BMW, German automaker noted for quality sports sedans and motorcycles and one of the most prominent brands in the world. 
Headquarters are in Munich.
It originated in 1916 as Bayerische Flugzeug-Werke, a builder of aircraft engines, but assumed the name Bayerische Motoren Werke in July 1917 and began producing motorcycles in the 1920s. 
BMW entered the automobile business in 1928. The company’s R32 motorcycle set a world speed record that was not broken until 1937. During World War II BMW built the world’s first jet airplane engines, used by the Luftwaffe, Germany’s air force. After the war the company tried to move into the small-car market but found that it could not compete effectively against Volkswagen’s compact, inexpensive autos. By 1959 the company was on the verge of bankruptcy, and its managers were planning to sell the firm to Daimler-Benz.
In that year, however, BMW pulled out of its financial slump; German entrepreneur Herbert Quandt acquired a controlling interest in the firm, and BMW introduced its 700 series, soon followed by the equally successful 1500 model. 
At about the same time, the company introduced a new series of motorcycles that were particularly popular in the United States.
BMW was firmly established as a premium automobile brand by the end of the 20th century. In a failed attempt to gain market share as a sport-utility vehicle company, BMW purchased the Rover Group in 1994 but lost roughly $4 billion before selling the Land Rover brand to Ford in 2000. 
BMW saw great success with the relaunch of the British MINI in 2001, however, and another British brand, Rolls-Royce, became part of BMW in 2003. Members of the Quandt family continued to hold a significant stake in the company.
luxury, word that implies a relatively large consumption of wealth for nonessential pleasures. There is, however, no absolute definition of luxury, for the conception is relative to both time and person. It is a commonplace of history that the luxuries of one generation may become the necessities of a later period; thus, no hard and fast line can be drawn between luxuries, comforts, and necessities. The private bath was one of the greatest luxuries of the Roman Empire; in the 19th century its use was largely confined to the wealthy; today it may be ranked among the necessities of the developed world. Examined from one important standpoint, luxury might be defined as any expenditure in excess of the normal and customary standard of living of the class to which an individual belongs.

Following World War I, the Treaty of Versailles banned Germany from producing aircraft engines. To survive, BMW shifted its focus to railway brakes and motorcycle engines. In 1923, BMW released its first motorcycle, the R 32. Designed by Max Friz, it featured a horizontally opposed "boxer" engine and a highly reliable shaft drive—technologies that remain signature characteristics of BMW motorcycles to this day. 
BMW entered the automobile manufacturing industry in 1928 by acquiring Fahrzeugfabrik Eisenach, a company that built small cars under a license from the British Austin Motor Company. By 1932, BMW was designing and building its own original cars. During the 1930s and World War II, the company was heavily integrated into the German war economy, focusing once again on aircraft engines and utilizing forced labor in its production facilities—a dark chapter the company has since acknowledged and addressed in historical disclosures. 
The post-war years were difficult for BMW, but the introduction of the "New Class" sedans in the early 1960s saved the company from financial ruin and firmly established its reputation for building sporty, driver-focused vehicles. This era birthed the brand's enduring slogan, "The Ultimate Driving Machine." 
In the modern era, the BMW Group operates as a major luxury automotive conglomerate. Aside from its core BMW brand, the company acquired the British Rover Group in 1994 (though it later sold most of it off, retaining only the MINI brand) and secured the rights to Rolls-Royce Motor Cars in 1998. In the 21st century, BMW has been a pioneer in electric mobility, launching the carbon-fiber-bodied BMW i3 in 2013. 
The company currently maintains a massive global manufacturing network, with its largest plant located in Spartanburg, South Carolina, USA, where it produces the majority of its highly successful "X" series SUVs.
